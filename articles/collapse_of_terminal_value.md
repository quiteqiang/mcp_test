### What Happens If AI Makes Every Moat Temporary?

## Core Thesis

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Central Question** | What if AI disruption is so fast and broad that no company can project cash flows beyond 5 years? |
| **Key Assumption Challenged** | Modern capital markets assume competitive advantages (moats, brands, network effects) compound over time |
| **Proposed Outcome** | Equities get repriced not on long-term DCF, but on near-term FCF multiples (2–7x instead of 22–60x today) |

---

## The Disruption Repricing Framework

| Annual Disruption Probability | Implied FCF Multiple | Implied Business Lifespan |
|-------------------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| 10% per year | ~6.5x FCF | ~10 years |
| 20% per year | ~3.9x FCF | ~5 years |
| 30% per year | ~2.8x FCF | ~3 years |

> **Baseline:** A stable, no-growth business deserves 10–12x FCF today (based on ~4.5% risk-free rate + 4–5% equity risk premium).

## Historical Precedents

| Industry | Period | Multiple Compression | Cause |
|----------|--------|----------------------|-------|
| Newspapers | 2005–2015 | 12–15x → 2–4x EBITDA | Digital advertising killed print model |
| Retail | 2016–2020 | Compressed to 3–6x FCF | Amazon dismantled brick-and-mortar economics |
| Energy (Oil) | 2019–2021 | 4–6x FCF | Markets priced stranded asset risk from energy transition |
| NYC Taxi Medallions | 2013–2018 | $1M+ → <$100K | Uber made the cash flow endpoint visible |

## Second & Third Order Effects

| Domain | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Growth Investing** | Dies — no terminal value to grow into; VC/growth equity becomes a historical artifact |
| **IPO Market** | Collapses to only cash-generating businesses; story-based valuations cease |
| **Stock Options** | Lose power; employees demand cash over equity |
| **Capital Allocation** | Rotates to physical, AI-resistant assets |
| **R&D / CapEx** | Shortens dramatically; 7-year+ bets become unfundable |
| **M&A** | Inverts — acquirers demand immediate FCF accretion, stop paying for optionality |

---

## Where Capital Would Flee

| Asset Class | Why It Survives |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Energy infrastructure | Inelastic demand, physical defensibility |
| Farmland | Cannot be disrupted by a better LLM |
| Toll roads / water rights | Durable, tangible cash flows |
| Commodity producers | Physical assets, essential demand |
| Short-duration sovereign bonds | No terminal value assumption needed |
| Gold | Classic store of value in uncertainty |

---

## The Paradox: AI Disrupts Itself

> The companies driving AI spend **$300–500B/year** on infrastructure — capital that only makes sense with 7–15 year return horizons.
> In a 2–7x FCF world, that long-term capex becomes **unfinanceable**.
> AI development slows. Disruption slows. Moats return. The repricing reverses.

---

## Nation-States Fill the Void

| Who Benefits | Why |
|--------------|-----|
| US, China, Gulf States, Norway, Singapore | High savings rates + sovereign capital can invest on 20–30 year horizons |
| State capitalism model | Vindicated precisely when private market pricing breaks down |
| Industrial policy | Stops being fringe — becomes essential infrastructure strategy |

## Most Likely Outcome

Not a permanent new regime, but an **oscillating transition**:
- Shorter cycles
- Fatter tails
- Higher volatility
- Periodic crises of confidence in terminal values, followed by recoveries
- Structurally higher equity risk premium
- The comfortable long-run upward drift of equity markets becomes **something you earn, not assume**
